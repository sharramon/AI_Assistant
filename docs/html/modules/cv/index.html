cv: tailor a CV for one job with Rezi, one clean page, your yes

Purpose

Takes a CV as a PDF (any .pdf) and a job description (JD) as text. Rezi edits and scores a new copy for that JD. The module's worker reformats it to one clean page (US Letter by default), has Rezi check that page, and returns the one-page PDF you approved, with the scores, keywords and checks that led to it. One module with its worker inside; it stands alone, and other modules (the job agent) connect to it later through cv.task.

How it works inside: Architecture. Its menu, and what a job sends in and gets back: Tools.

In and out

  • In (cv.task): the job's company and role, the job description as text or as a .txt, .md or .html file, and the CV file: the path of any existing .pdf, in any folder and with any name, used as given (a non-.pdf, an .odt too, is refused, and so is a missing or 0-byte file; since 2026-11-13 there's no cv_dir or <cv_prefix> rule for it). Optional: the posting's URL, Education first, a target score, notes.
  • Out (cv.read_result on the task's result): the new one-page PDF and report.md (in the job's folder), and with them the Rezi Score after each round against the target, the keywords still missing and those left out because you don't have them, recommendations, the one-page check, and the compare of what Rezi read. Also the new Rezi resume and its check copy, and whether you approved it.

The calls and every field: Tools.

The design: how the module works once the CV Tool build is in. What is live today is in Build state at the end. The colours in the diagrams mark the same state (2026-11-09): green built and live, yellow built but not live, grey dashed not built.

The CV Tool is the cv module's own UI half. The module owns it: it launches the app, feeds it jobs and stops it. It isn't part of the Main Assistant or the core. The app's code sits in the module's folder as cv/app/cv_tool/. app/ is no Python package: the core puts every module's app/ folder on the import path at startup, so the app keeps its own name (python -m cv_tool) and never imports the module's code, and CompanionApp passes that folder on to the app's process (PYTHONPATH).

How cv_agent reaches Rezi today

There are two ways, and both are live:

  • Rezi's MCP API, through the cv module. The rezi.* items (mcp.py) call https://api.rezi.ai/mcp with your OAuth sign-in token. That covers rezi.write_resume (make or update a resume this module made), rezi.read_resume, rezi.generate_keywords, and rezi.list_resumes. No browser is involved.
  • Chrome, through the Claude in Chrome extension (chrome = true in module.toml). The prompt allows it only on app.rezi.ai, signed in as you. cv_agent uses it to read the Rezi Score, its categories, and the matched and missing keywords (prompt step 4), and for AI Keyword Targeting.

The CV Tool app never talks to Rezi itself (memory note cv-tool-rezi-request.md).

You watch Rezi in your own Chrome. cv_agent drives Rezi through the Claude in Chrome extension, in your normal Chrome, where you're signed in to Rezi. You look at that Chrome window to see what it's doing. The CV Tool shows no live view of Rezi.

The 5 steps

The worker's prompt (worker.py) follows the CV Tool's tabs. They replace the old 8 steps (built; live with the cv reload).

  1. Set up, in code: a job comes in through the Hub (cv.task, then hub.start to cv_agent). Before the worker sees it, the gate makes work\<date>_<Company>_<Role> in the worker's workspace, copies the base CV and the JD into it, and puts the rules folder and the confirmed facts into the task in full. The worker says the job is set up and waits until you tell it to start.
  2. Job & CV: cv.open_tool starts the CV Tool, opens the job in it (the JD, and the base CV's content read from the copy) and gives the app's job id. Text the reader couldn't place goes to you as a question.
  3. Pick: Internal or Rezi, only one. The request's tool decides; else you pick in the CV Tool's tab 2.
  4. First Draft: the worker makes true edits to the CV's content and sends them with cv.tool_round, which prints the local PDF and checks it against 9 pt. With Rezi, it writes the job's Rezi resume, opens it in your Chrome and keeps that tab, reads the score and keywords, and sends them in the same way. Then it stops for your review.
  5. ATS Loop: true edits each round, a new local PDF, the score read again. It stops at 90 or more, after at most 5 rounds, or when only terms your CVs don't back are left. Such a term comes to you as a question. Text under 9 pt is shortened or asked about, never shrunk.
  6. Submit: yours, in the CV Tool. It writes CV_Test_<Company>_final.pdf and result.json. A Submit is never refused: text under 9 pt only shows a warning on screen and in the message. The cv module sees the Submit on the CV Tool's event stream and tells the job's asker itself (DeliverMessage), in the same second. The worker doesn't wait for it: when the CV is ready it tells you so and ends its task with CV_RESULT status ready (everything but your Submit), and hears of your Submit from one [cv] line at its next task (design A, 2026-11-10).

The code behind it is drawn on the Architecture page.

Who does what

Who Does
Rezi (MCP + web app) A scorer only, with the Rezi pick: holds a new resume for each job (the base is never changed), and its score, categories and matched and missing keywords are read in your Chrome. Its PDF is never downloaded, and its AI writer isn't used.
The CV Tool (cv/app/cv_tool, its own window) Shows the four tabs, prints the CV's own page as the local PDF, scores it with the Internal pick, keeps the rounds, and holds your Submit.
The worker (cv_agent) The editor and the truth gate: keeps only wording backed by facts on your CVs or the confirmed facts, adds no skill, title, tool or number you don't have, no keyword stuffing. Drives the CV Tool through the cv.open_tool / cv.tool_* items and Rezi through the rezi.* items and Chrome. Asks you before starting and after the First Draft; tells you when it's ready for your Submit and ends its task. Never uploads or submits anything outside Rezi.
You Say when to start, pick the tool if the request didn't, review the First Draft, and press Submit.
